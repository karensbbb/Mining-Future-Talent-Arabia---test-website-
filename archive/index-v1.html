The mandate

Ore bodies are the easy part. People are the constraint.

Saudi Arabia has the geology, the capital and the licensing regime. What it is short of — and what every operator, contractor and investor in the sector is competing for — is proven mining talent on the ground.

Mining is now the third pillar of Saudi industry alongside oil and petrochemicals. Phosphate and bauxite at Waad al-Shamal and Al-Baitha, gold across the Arabian Shield, copper and zinc at Jabal Sayid, and a fast-growing downstream base in Ras al-Khair and Jubail have created demand for skills that simply did not exist at scale in the region ten years ago.

The shortage is specific, not general. There is no shortage of CVs. There is a shortage of resource geologists who have signed off a reportable estimate, of drill-and-blast engineers who have worked a hard-rock bench, of metallurgists who have commissioned a flotation circuit, and of shift supervisors who can hold a safety standard through a ramp-up.

We are built around that gap. Our mining practice recruits against technical competency and operating environment — not job titles — and we do it with the nationalisation and local-content obligations of Saudi projects designed into the plan from the start.

Across the mine lifecycle

Different stage, different people.

A project needs a different workforce at every stage of its life. We work with clients from first licence through to closure, and the brief changes each time.

  1. Exploration

    Small, mobile, highly technical teams. Camp-based geology, drilling supervision and data discipline from day one.

    Project geologists · Drill supervisors · Camp & logistics

  2. Studies & permitting

    Scoping through feasibility. Study managers and specialist consultants who can produce a bankable document and defend it.

    Study managers · Competent Persons · Environmental & social

  3. Development

    Construction and pre-production. Owner’s team, contract management and the first operating hires brought in ahead of handover.

    Owner’s team · Construction & contracts · Pre-production

  4. Ramp-up & operations

    The volume stage. Full rosters mobilised against the production schedule, with commissioning specialists overlapping the operators.

    Full rosters · Commissioning · Shift leadership

  5. Expansion & closure

    Brownfield growth, optimisation and eventual rehabilitation — including the redeployment of people the operation has trained.

    Optimisation · Debottlenecking · Rehabilitation & redeployment

Saudization & local content

Hire for the target. Build for the decade.

A Saudi mining operation is measured on more than production. Nationalisation rates, local content and in-Kingdom capability are licence conditions, tender criteria and board-level reporting lines. We plan for them in the same breath as the roster.

  • Saudi national pipelines. Graduate, apprentice and technical-institute sourcing built around the disciplines your site will actually be short of in three years’ time.
  • Deliberate knowledge transfer. Expatriate specialists engaged with a named successor, a competency framework and a handover horizon written into the mandate.
  • Returning and diaspora talent. Saudi professionals with international mining experience — the fastest route to senior national leadership on site.
  • Compliance you can report. Nationalisation tracking, localisation reporting and workforce documentation prepared for regulators, lenders and JV partners.
