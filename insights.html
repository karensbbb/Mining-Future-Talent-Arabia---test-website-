From the mining desk.

Packages, nationalisation, ramp up rosters and the disciplines getting harder to hire. Written by the consultants running the searches.

What Saudi mining operations pay technical staff

Every first call asks what the role is worth. In Saudi mining, base salary is the least telling part of the package, and comparing base alone disappoints both sides.

What moves the number

  • Residential or rotational. Family status, single status and FIFO packages for the same role are not comparable on base.
  • Housing and schooling. On family status offers they often outweigh a base increase. Offers are won and lost here.
  • Commodity and method. Underground hard rock commands a premium over surface work at the same level. The regional pool is far thinner.
  • Statutory competencies. A Registered Mine Manager ticket, or Competent Person status under JORC or SAMREC, is a step change, not an increment.
  • Stage of operation. Commissioning and ramp up roles pay a premium over steady state equivalents, with the least time to fill.

Where the gaps are widest

Processing and metallurgy, where international candidates benchmark against Australian or Canadian packages. And mid level technical services, where the market has outrun most internal salary bands.

Saudisation in mining: meet the target, keep the plant

In mining, nationalisation is a workforce design problem, not a quota. Run it as a capability transfer programme with a schedule attached.

The failure mode

An operation hires to the percentage with no development path. A few years on, the target is met on paper and a handful of expatriates are still the only people who can run the plant. Target met, risk created.

What works instead

  • Name the successor in the mandate. Every senior expatriate hire comes with a successor and a handover horizon, written into the brief.
  • Build the pipeline three years ahead. Graduate, apprentice and technical institute intakes for the disciplines you will be short of then, not now.
  • Go after returning talent. Saudi professionals with international mining experience are the fastest route to national technical leadership, and the most contested people in the market.
  • Make the competency framework real. Assessed, signed off progression, not time served. Hold it to the standard of a statutory ticket.

Why ramp up rosters slip, and how to fix them a year earlier

Projects rarely miss nameplate because the plant will not run. They miss it because the people who can run it are not there yet, and those hiring decisions are made before first blast.

The three compressions

  • Construction and operations overlap. Commissioning specialists and first crews must be on site before construction finishes, hired against a schedule still moving.
  • Senior technical lead time. A General Manager or Head of Technical Services search takes months, not weeks. Start it when the plant is nearly ready and it is already late.
  • Mobilisation is not instant. Medicals, police clearances, visa, iqama and travel add weeks per person, exactly when the schedule has no slack.

What to do about it

Turn the manning schedule into a hiring schedule, built backwards from first ore with mobilisation lead times included. Start senior searches a year out; the carrying cost is small next to a delayed ramp up. Run volume hiring as campaigns, not a queue of requisitions.
