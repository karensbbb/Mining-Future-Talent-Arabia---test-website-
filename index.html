The mandate

Ore bodies are the easy part. People are the constraint.

Saudi Arabia has the geology, the capital and the licensing regime. What it is short of — and what every operator, contractor and investor in the sector is competing for — is proven mining talent on the ground.

Your competitors are mobilising right now. The question is whether the people who know how to run the plant are joining their roster or yours.

3 of 4

senior mining roles in the Gulf are filled by people who were not looking when they were approached. They are employed, on a roster they know, and they will not answer an advert. That is a search problem, not an advertising problem — and it is the one we are built for.

Mining is now the third pillar of Saudi industry alongside oil and petrochemicals. Phosphate and bauxite at Waad al-Shamal and Al-Baitha, gold across the Arabian Shield, copper and zinc at Jabal Sayid, and a fast-growing downstream base in Ras al-Khair and Jubail have created demand for skills that simply did not exist at scale in the region ten years ago.

The shortage is specific, not general. There is no shortage of CVs. There is a shortage of resource geologists who have signed off a reportable estimate, of drill-and-blast engineers who have worked a hard-rock bench, of metallurgists who have commissioned a flotation circuit, and of shift supervisors who can hold a safety standard through a ramp-up.

We are built around that gap. Our mining practice recruits against technical competency and operating environment — not job titles — and we do it with the nationalisation and local-content obligations of Saudi projects designed into the plan from the start.

Saudization & local content

Hire for the target. Build for the decade.

A Saudi mining operation is measured on more than production. Nationalisation rates, local content and in-Kingdom capability are licence conditions, tender criteria and board-level reporting lines. We plan for them in the same breath as the roster.

  • Saudi national pipelines. Graduate, apprentice and technical-institute sourcing built around the disciplines your site will actually be short of in three years' time.
  • Deliberate knowledge transfer. Expatriate specialists engaged with a named successor, a competency framework and a handover horizon written into the mandate.
  • Returning and diaspora talent. Saudi professionals with international mining experience — the fastest route to senior national leadership on site.
  • Compliance you can report. Nationalisation tracking, localisation reporting and workforce documentation prepared for regulators, lenders and JV partners.

Common questions

Before you send the brief.

Do you only recruit in Saudi Arabia?

Saudi Arabia is where most of the current demand sits, and it is where our mining practice is deepest — but we work across the GCC, including industrial minerals and quarrying in the UAE and Oman, and downstream processing throughout the Gulf.

We source globally. A resource geologist for a Saudi gold operation is as likely to come from Perth, Johannesburg or Santiago as from the region itself.

How do you charge?

It depends on the mandate. Retained search for leadership and scarce technical roles runs on a staged retainer. Volume mobilisation and contract staffing are priced per placement or on a margin against the contract rate.

Either way you see the commercial model in writing before work starts, and the market view that precedes it costs nothing.

What happens if a placement does not work out?

Permanent placements carry a replacement guarantee, with the period set by the seniority of the role and agreed in the terms of business up front. If a hire leaves inside it, we run the search again at no additional fee.

Can you help us hit Saudization targets without losing capability?

That is the core of the problem, and it is why we treat it as workforce planning rather than a hiring quota. We build the national pipeline — graduates, apprentices, technical institutes and returning diaspora talent — alongside the expatriate hires, with knowledge transfer, named successors and a handover horizon written into the mandate.

We are pre-production. Is it too early to talk?

It is usually too late rather than too early. Owner's team, technical services and the first operating hires need to be in place well ahead of handover, and the senior technical market moves slowly — a General Manager or Head of Technical Services can be a six-month search from first conversation to site.

I am a candidate. Will you keep my details confidential?

Yes. Your CV is never sent to a client without your explicit permission for that specific role, and we do not approach your current employer. This is a small market and we work in it every day — discretion is self-interest as much as policy.
